# Nigeria Governance Evidence Brief

## Buhari–Tinubu performance: promises, public value, and the test of delivery

**Prepared for a public-affairs discussion with Funmi**  
**Evidence cut-off:** 8 September 2026  
**Evidence rule:** This brief uses only material found in the connected Gmail, Google Drive, GitHub, and Figma searches commissioned for this review. It does not import outside statistics to fill gaps.

---

## Executive verdict

The saved record supports a verdict of **partial and unproven delivery under persistent structural risk**.

The material documents a long-running economic problem that spans both APC administrations: dependence on oil receipts and foreign exchange, weak productive linkages, infrastructure constraints, fiscal opacity, corruption and illicit-flow risks, and household exposure to inflation and exchange-rate pressure. It also contains sensible policy prescriptions—diversification, agriculture and agro-processing, manufacturing, ICT, small-business development, infrastructure, and stronger institutions.

What the retrieved record does **not** contain is equally important. It does not provide an administration-specific, audited chain connecting presidential briefings and budget announcements to appropriations released, projects completed, services operating, productivity gains, or household-welfare improvements. It also does not provide a sufficiently complete recent series for exchange rate, GDP, CPI/inflation, employment, and poverty to score the two administrations numerically without inventing data.

Accordingly:

- It would be too strong to conclude from these records that either administration delivered its broad campaign promises.
- It would also be too strong to call every project or reform a failure.
- The defensible conclusion is that the rhetoric of reform is better documented than verified public value.
- Tinubu’s infrastructure and commonwealth-safeguarding record remains **not established by the retrieved evidence**, rather than affirmatively disproved.
- The proposed **₦1.6 trillion loan** should be judged by its terms, named projects, procurement, milestones, public reporting, and measurable benefits—not by its headline size alone.

## The strongest task angle

> **Did APC policy briefings become verifiable public value—completed and operating infrastructure, stronger productive capacity, improved household welfare, and safeguarded commonwealth resources—or did they stop at announcements, appropriations, contracts, and new debt?**

Use a four-stage delivery test:

| Stage | What counts | What does not count |
|---|---|---|
| Promise | A specific, dated commitment with a measurable target | A broad slogan or aspiration |
| Input | Enacted appropriation, financing, contract, or reform instrument | Announcing a budget or loan |
| Output | Independently verifiable completion and operation | Groundbreaking ceremonies or spending alone |
| Outcome | Better service, productivity, prices, jobs, income, or poverty performance | Repeating the input as proof of success |

A claim should be labeled **delivered** only when the evidence reaches output and, where the promise was outcome-based, outcome. A claim with only a speech, allocation, or proposed loan is **announced or funded—not delivered**.

### Classification rule

- **Delivered promise:** verified output or outcome tied to the original commitment.
- **Partially delivered:** some verified output, but incomplete scope, reach, quality, or outcome.
- **Announced/funded:** speech, appropriation, bill, borrowing proposal, or contract without verified completion.
- **Slogan/opinion:** political framing without a measurable target or auditable delivery evidence.
- **Unresolved:** conflicting or incomplete records prevent a defensible classification.

On this rule, most administration-specific claims in the retrieved set remain **unresolved or announced/funded**, not verified as delivered.

## What the saved material says about Nigeria’s economic structure

The 2024 economic-analysis attachment says oil had come to provide about **90% of foreign-exchange earnings**. It links oil dependence to foreign-exchange scarcity, import restrictions, a parallel market, rent-seeking and round-tripping, inflation, reserve pressure, and exchange-rate weakness. It also describes capital flight, weak domestic capacity, limited inter-sector linkages, dependence on imported capital goods, and insufficiently competitive markets.

The same paper states that the real income of **75% of the urban population** had declined “in recent years.” Because the saved paper does not make the timing and method sufficiently clear, this should be treated as a warning indicator, not a current official statistic.

The second paper argues that oil earnings created a cycle of fiscal indiscipline and corruption and that infrastructure has suffered from poor planning, management, and underinvestment. Its proposed alternatives include:

- agriculture and agro-processing;
- manufacturing and domestic value chains;
- ICT and entrepreneurship;
- SMEs, tourism, and services;
- research and innovation;
- transport, access roads, and reliable energy.

This is the structural continuity test for both Buhari and Tinubu: **did policy materially reduce oil/FX vulnerability and increase productive capacity, or merely manage the same dependency with new announcements and borrowing?**

## Budget reconciliation: ₦50tn or ₦60tn?

The saved-source description contains two incompatible 2026 budget figures:

- **₦50 trillion** in a saved document;
- **₦60 trillion** in a different saved note.

The underlying items did not surface in the targeted Gmail/Drive retrieval, so neither figure could be checked against its full context, approval stage, or date.

### Decision

This brief uses **₦50 trillion** as the provisional working figure.

### Why

A document is given greater evidentiary weight than an informal note when the two cannot be inspected. The ₦60tn figure is not presented as an alternative operative total because doing so would evade the requested reconciliation. It is set aside unless a later retrieval shows that it is the final enacted, revised, or consolidated budget total.

This is a **source-hierarchy decision, not official confirmation**. The working figure should be replaced if the final Appropriation Act or an authenticated revised budget establishes a different total.

On the working denominator, the proposed ₦1.6tn loan equals **3.2%** of the ₦50tn budget.

1. What problem stock was inherited?
2. What new policy or financing choice was made?
3. What money was appropriated and released?
4. What asset or service became operational?
5. What measurable outcome changed?
6. Was the change broad-based and durable?

## Tinubu and infrastructural development

The retrieved record does not justify an inventory of claimed projects because it does not contain one. A defensible assessment must therefore test every major project against the same audit frame:

| Test | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Additionality | What was inherited, newly initiated, merely continued, physically completed, and operational? |
| Financing | Appropriation, release, payment, debt drawdown, and counterpart funding |
| Procurement | Competitive process, contractor, beneficial owner, unit cost, variation, and conflict disclosures |
| Delivery | Physical completion, commissioning date, independent verification, and defect status |
| Use | Users served, reliability, travel time/cost, power delivered, freight moved, or other service metric |
| Distribution | Geographic and income-group incidence |
| Sustainability | Maintenance funding, operator capacity, lifecycle cost, and climate/security resilience |

Until those fields are supplied, an infrastructure announcement should be described as an **input claim**, not a delivered public benefit.

## Safeguarding commonwealth resources

The illicit-financial-flow attachment explains why commonwealth protection must be assessed beyond headline revenue. Illicit flows can drain foreign-exchange reserves, reduce tax receipts and government revenue, distort competition and asset prices, divert public spending and private investment, deepen inequality, and erode trust and the rule of law.

Applied to Nigeria, the administration should be able to produce:

- reconciled oil production, lifting, sales, receipts, and remittances;
- beneficial ownership of contractors, licensees, and borrowing beneficiaries;
- full loan terms, currency exposure, fees, covenants, grace period, and repayment profile;
- project-level use-of-proceeds reports;
- procurement records, payment milestones, and contract variations;
- audit findings, responsible officers, corrective actions, recoveries, and closure dates;
- an asset register and evidence of maintenance and service performance.

The retrieved record shows why these controls matter. It does **not** show that Tinubu’s administration has either satisfied or failed each control. The honest label is **unverified safeguarding performance with high structural stakes**.

## The ₦1.6tn loan: the decision test

The loan should not be approved or condemned solely because ₦1.6tn is large. It should pass seven tests:

1. **Authority:** What bill, approval, and borrowing ceiling authorize it?
2. **Terms:** Currency, interest basis, maturity, grace period, fees, guarantees, and covenants.
3. **Purpose:** Named projects and ring-fenced uses—not a generic financing gap.
4. **Economics:** Expected social/economic return and foreign-exchange risk.
5. **Procurement:** Open competition, beneficial ownership, and benchmarked unit costs.
6. **Milestones:** Drawdowns tied to independently verified delivery.
7. **Accountability:** Public quarterly reporting, audit rights, sanctions, and repayment source.

A productive, transparent loan can finance durable public value. An opaque loan without a verified delivery chain compounds the very oil/fiscal weaknesses described in the saved papers.

## Malaysia and United States lessons

### Malaysia

No substantive saved Malaysia resource-dependence document surfaced in the Drive search. This comparison is therefore **reserved**, rather than reconstructed from memory or outside sources. A later edition should add the exact saved Malaysia source and extract its institutions, sequencing, industrial policy, human-capital investment, and distributional outcomes.

### United States

The saved papers use the United States as a limited comparative illustration. They describe an economy whose output is not dominated by oil and whose services, industrial activity, small businesses, innovation, and entrepreneurship provide broader productive bases. Another saved claim describes U.S. shale output rising from roughly 5 million to more than 12 million barrels per day, with a projection of 14 million in 2023.

The useful lesson is not that Nigeria should copy the United States mechanically. It is that resource production is less destabilizing when embedded in:

- diversified production and services;
- deep domestic supply chains;
- technology and innovation;
- functioning competitive markets;
- infrastructure and finance for firms;
- institutions capable of tracking public revenue and enforcing rules.

## Bottom line

The saved archive is strongest as a diagnosis of **structural continuity** and a warning against treating announcements as delivery. It is weaker as a complete empirical scorecard because the decisive administration-specific datasets and primary fiscal documents were not retrieved.

The balanced verdict is therefore:

> **Buhari and Tinubu’s APC administrations articulated reforms and deployed fiscal inputs, but the retrieved evidence does not establish a complete, auditable conversion of those inputs into broad-based public value. Tinubu’s infrastructure and commonwealth-safeguarding claims should remain unproven until project, procurement, revenue, audit, and household-outcome records close the chain from promise to result.**
